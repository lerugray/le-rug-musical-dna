In practice, the majority of the catalog lives in a blurred zone between power chord and sus2 — the ambiguity is the default voicing, not a color applied on top.

## Beyond Power Chords: Your Color Palette

### Major Triads (13.8%)
C major (3,200), D major (2,954), G major (2,896), E major (1,301), A major (1,198), F major (1,120)

Your major triads follow a C-D-G pattern — the "bright" keys. When you commit to a full triad instead of a power chord, you go major more than minor (2.1:1 ratio).

### Minor Triads (6.7%)
A minor (2,023) leads, followed by E minor (1,641) and B minor (973). You reach for A minor when you want darkness — it's your most reliable minor shape.

### Suspended Chords (10.9% combined — actually much higher)
**Sus4:** A sus4 (1,974) dominates. D sus4 (935) follows.
**Sus2:** C sus2 (2,538) is massive — your single most-used non-power-chord shape. A sus2 (2,139) follows.

The 10.9% figure only counts chords the detector explicitly classified as sus2/sus4. Given that The Standard power chord shape already includes the 2nd, the real sus2 presence across the catalog is much higher — likely overlapping with the majority of the 53.0% power chord figure. The C sus2 dominance (2,538 instances) is the detector catching the cases where the 2nd is prominent enough to override the power chord classification, but it's present in some form almost everywhere.

## What This Tells Us

You have a **three-tier chord system:**

1. **Power chords** — your default, your comfort zone, the wall of sound
2. **Sus chords** — your movement chords, creating ambiguity and motion within a section
3. **Major/minor triads** — deployed selectively for emotional emphasis

Most "theory-trained" indie guitarists would reverse tiers 1 and 3 — using full triads as the default and power chords for emphasis. Your inversion of this is part of your sound. By keeping the harmony ambiguous most of the time and only revealing major/minor at key moments, you create a sense of constant forward motion — the listener is always waiting for the chord to "resolve" into something definitive, and you only give them that resolution selectively.

With the full catalog counted (including BtC, Living Serious, solo material), the power chord percentage dropped from where Le Rug sits alone (~61%) to 53.0% overall — the other projects use slightly more harmonic variety, especially the solo instrumentals which lean into major triads and sus2 chords more freely.
