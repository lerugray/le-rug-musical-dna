## What the Data Shows

### Ray Is Exactly Where He Said He Was

Ray describes his sound as "an aggressive Built to Spill." The data confirms this almost mathematically:

**Chord vocabulary:** Ray's 57% power chords sits directly between The Fall's 94% and Built to Spill's 40%. He took the Fall's power-chord-dominant approach and added Built to Spill's harmonic richness (triads, 7ths, sus chords). His 14% major triads are halfway between the Fall's 4% and BtS's 26%.

**Density:** Ray's 7.0 n/s is between The Fall's sparse 1.4 and BtS's dense 10.9. Again, the midpoint.

**Tempo:** Ray's 198 BPM is close to BtS's 210 — both playing at punk speed. The Fall is significantly slower at 124 BPM. Ray took the Fall's aesthetic but plays it at Built to Spill's velocity.
![Chart](https://lerugray.github.io/le-rug-musical-dna/images/Influence-Fingerprint-Comparison-2.png)


### The Beefheart Connection Is Real — and It's Sus2/Sus4

Captain Beefheart's fingerprint is the most surprising. His chord breakdown: 79% power chords, **5.8% sus2, 5.6% sus4**. Almost identical sus2/sus4 balance.

Ray's catalog: 57% power chords, **5.4% sus2, 5.2% sus4**.

The Fall: 94% power chords, **0% sus2, 0% sus4**. Zero suspended chords.
Built to Spill: 40% power chords, **4.5% sus2, 0% sus4**. Sus2 but no sus4.

**The harmonic ambiguity — the equal sus2/sus4 balance that creates the constant push-pull — comes from Beefheart, not from The Fall or Built to Spill.** The Fall doesn't use suspended chords at all. Built to Spill uses sus2 but not sus4. Only Beefheart has the balanced sus2/sus4 that defines Ray's sound.

This maps perfectly to what Ray said: Beefheart is the reason dissonance never needed to resolve. The suspended-chord ambiguity — the defining feature that separates Le Rug from a standard power-chord punk band — traces to Beefheart, not to the other two influences.

### The Fall Gave Ray the Philosophy, Not the Sound

The Fall's fingerprint is almost nothing like Le Rug's by the numbers:
- 94% power chords vs Ray's 57%
- 1.4 n/s vs Ray's 7.0
- 124 BPM vs Ray's 198
- 30% repeated notes vs Ray's 10%

The Fall is sparse, slow, repetitive, and harmonically simple. Le Rug is dense, fast, varied, and harmonically richer. What Ray took from The Fall isn't audible in the chord analysis or the density numbers — it's structural: the idea that atonal ugliness can be hammered into pop through conviction and repetition. That's a creative philosophy, not a chord vocabulary.

The one metric where The Fall and Le Rug match closely: **bass-heavy register** (The Fall 64%, Le Rug 59%). Both play low.

### Built to Spill Gave Ray the Palette

Built to Spill's fingerprint maps closely to Le Rug's in several ways:
- Both use major triads prominently (BtS 26%, Le Rug 14%)
- Both have sub-5% sus2 usage
- Similar register distributions (BtS 56% bass, Le Rug 59%)
- Nearly identical ascending/descending balance (BtS 46%/47%, Le Rug 45%/45%)

**The melodic movement is the clearest match.** Both zigzag nearly equally between ascending and descending. The Fall, by contrast, repeats notes 30% of the time — a fundamentally different melodic approach (drones and repetition vs oscillation).

But the Suggested Listening page already noted the key difference: Built to Spill's melodies arc over long phrases (Doug Martsch writes climbers); Ray's melodies oscillate in place (the zigzag). Same notes, different movement. The ascending/descending percentages are similar, but the *pattern* (3-note contour) would differ. That's a subtlety the current analysis captures directionally but could be explored further.

### Where Ray Sits

| Trait | Inherited From | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Power chord foundation | The Fall + Beefheart | Both >79% power chords |
| Harmonic ambiguity (sus2/sus4) | **Beefheart** | Only Beefheart has balanced sus2/sus4 |
| Triads and 7ths for color | Built to Spill | BtS 26% major, 10% maj7 |
| Tempo and density | Built to Spill | Both 200+ BPM, high n/s |
| Bass-heavy register | The Fall | Both 60%+ below E3 |
| Atonal-to-pop philosophy | The Fall | Not measurable in chords, but structural |
| Melodic zigzag | Shared (BtS + Beefheart) | Both have ~45/45 ascending/descending |
| Non-resolution | **Beefheart** | 0% dominant 7ths, 0% blues voicings |
