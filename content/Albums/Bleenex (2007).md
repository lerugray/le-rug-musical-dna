# Bleenex (2007)

**13 tracks** | **38.5 min** | **202 BPM avg** | **8.3 n/s**

The debut Le Rug record. Produced and mixed by **John DeNicola** (who wrote "(I've Had) The Time of My Life" from Dirty Dancing) and Ray Weiss at Jake's Kitchen Sink Recording Studio, NYC. Files recovered directly from DeNicola, 2026-04-09.

## The Surprise

**Bleenex is your second-densest album at 8.3 n/s** — right behind Anyone But Hindenburg (9.1) and ahead of Dead In A Hole (8.9). This demolishes the assumption that you got denser over time. You started dense, got sparser in the middle period, and came back to density later.

Even more striking: **Gloss opens the album at 17.2 n/s** — matching Indefinite Diagnosis as the densest single track in your entire catalog. Your very first track on your very first album was already at your absolute ceiling. You didn't build toward that intensity — you started there.

## Key Center: A-Centered

**A is the dominant pitch class** (2,479), followed by G, F, E, C. This is unique — no other Le Rug album is A-centered. A is the 5th open string; centering here rather than E (6th string) or G (3rd string) gives a brighter, mid-range foundation. The prominence of F (1,948) is also unusual — F is your least-used note in most later records.

The 5th interval (7 semitones) is proportionally much higher here than on later records — nearly matching octaves. You were doing more raw power chord movement and less octave doubling on the debut.

## Dynamic Range

The density swings on Bleenex are extreme: 17.2 (Gloss) to 3.9 (Gaxinthaw) — a **4.4x ratio** within the first two tracks. No other album has this kind of dynamic range in its sequencing. You open at maximum intensity, immediately pull back to nearly nothing, then build through the middle before the dense Amp-Dress-Parade-Hindenburg closing run.

This is sophisticated sequencing for a debut — whether intentional or intuitive, it creates a listening arc that most bands take years to learn.

## Notes

Telebones at 5:42 is notable — it would be by far the longest Le Rug track until "You Tried" on the Ersatz EP (4:24) and the Anyone But Hindenburg tracks. Worth analyzing once recovered to see if the longer format changed the compositional approach.

Ray plays all instruments except drums on some tracks (Matthew Gaffney). **Double drums** — two drummers, a configuration borrowed from The Fall's Karl Burns / Paul Hanley setup.
