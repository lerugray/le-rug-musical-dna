# Evolution Over Time

*How your writing changed from 2007 to 2020 — across all projects.*

## The Arc

The old narrative was simple: you started sparse and got denser over time. **Bleenex blows that up.** Your debut album in 2007 was already at 8.3 n/s — denser than anything you'd release again until Dead In A Hole in 2014. You didn't build toward intensity. You started there, pulled back, and came back to it.

### Phase 0: Before the Data (~2003-2006)
**Project:** The Medics — *Shangri-La*

No analysis data yet (record still with the producer), but the context matters: Ray was writing and recording by 16, already filtering Captain Beefheart and The Fall through a guitar he played entirely by ear. By the time Bleenex arrived in 2007, the instincts were already locked in — Phase 1 isn't really the beginning, it's just the earliest thing we can measure.

### Phase 1: The Debut — Dense From Day One (2007)
**Album:** Bleenex

- **Key centers:** A, G, F — unique in the catalog. A-centered, not E.
- **Density:** 8.3 n/s — your second-densest album
- **Tempo:** 202 BPM average
- **Character:** Produced by John DeNicola. Gloss opens at 17.2 n/s — tied for the densest single track in your entire catalog. Then Gaxinthaw drops to 3.9 n/s — a 4.4x dynamic swing in the first two tracks. The album shows sophisticated sequencing, extreme density, and a 5th-interval emphasis that later gives way to more octave doubling.

The power chord / octave doubling signature was already fully formed here. You didn't develop your style — you arrived with it.

### Phase 2: The Twin Albums (~2008)
**Albums:** Sex Reduction Flower, C.R.E.E.P.

- **Key centers:** G and C (SRF), A and E (C.R.E.E.P.)
- **Density:** SRF 6.6 n/s, C.R.E.E.P. 7.2 n/s — both noticeably sparser than Bleenex
- **Tempo:** 190-200 BPM
- **Character:** These share 5 tracks (Tripper, 2C-E, Sweet Kicks, Buffalo, Hugo) — two different packagings of the same creative period. SRF is brighter (G/C center), C.R.E.E.P. is darker (A/E center). The density drop from Bleenex isn't a decline — it's a pullback, room to breathe after the debut's wall of sound.

This is the period where the shoegaze influence is most audible in the data — open strings ringing, lower density allowing notes to sustain and interact.

### Phase 3: Experimentation (~2009-2010)
**Albums:** America, Nuke Whales

- **Key centers:** G#, D# (America — totally unusual); E, B, A (Nuke Whales)
- **Density:** America 4.7 n/s, Nuke Whales 6.1 n/s
- **Tempo:** 196-199 BPM
- **Character:** America is the oddball — the darkest key centers (G#/D#) and sparsest density in the Le Rug catalog. Nuke Whales is 44 songs about presidents, most under 60 seconds. Constraint-driven writing. William Henry Harrison = 5.5 seconds (20 notes). Ronald Reagan = longest at 109 seconds. You learned to write complete musical statements in miniature.

### Phase 4: The Outlier
**Album:** Sticky Buns (date unknown, possibly 2011-2013)

- **Key centers:** F# and C# — totally unique in your catalog
- **Density:** 3.5 n/s — by far your sparsest
- **Tempo:** 179 BPM — your slowest
- **Signature:** Unison intervals (#1) instead of octaves. Repetition-based, drone-like.

This is your most Fall-influenced record by the numbers — specifically the Hanley/Burns/Scanlon/Riley Fall, where atonal progressions get hammered into pop through sheer repetition and conviction. The unison repetition, the low density, the unusual key centers (F#/C# are NOT open-string keys — you were fretting more deliberately). If there's a "lost" aesthetic in your catalog that could be developed further, it's this one.

### Phase 5: Peak Aggression (2012-2014)
**Albums:** Dead In A Hole EP, Ersatz EP, Cut Off Your Dick And Turn Into Slime, Party With Peter Burns, Swelling (My Own Worst Anime)
**Other projects:** Butter the Children — self-titled EP (2012), True Crime (2014)

- **Key centers:** E, A (DIAH); C, G, E (Ersatz); D, G (COYDAT); G, B, D (True Crime); G, E, F# (Swelling)
- **Density:** DIAH 8.9, Ersatz 8.5, True Crime 9.2, COYDAT 6.4, PwPB 7.9, Swelling 6.7, BtC EP 6.7
- **Tempo:** 194-210 BPM
- **Character:** The busiest period. Le Rug goes fully solo (Ray plays everything from DIAH onward). True Crime with Butter the Children hits 9.2 n/s with a full band — the densest album-length recording until the full catalog recount. COYDAT is the palette cleanser: D-centered, 6.4 n/s, a brief drop before the next push. Jesse Harris produced True Crime; Pat Dillett engineered.

Swelling, recorded solo in Bangkok, is the most harmonically stripped-down Le Rug record: 81.1% power chords (highest in the catalog), 200 BPM, standard density. F# appearing as a key center across multiple tracks is unusual — suggesting different guitar positions than the open-string defaults. The Bangkok isolation didn't diminish the style; it concentrated it.

The BtC self-titled EP (2012) predates True Crime by two years — Ray wrote everything, played both guitar and drums, with Max Kagan on bass and Scott Andrews producing. The harmonic signature (A/D/E centers, 77.9% power chords) is fully his.

This is the "aggressive Built to Spill" at its most aggressive. Dead In A Hole's 8.9 n/s matches your debut's intensity — you came back to where you started.

### Phase 6: Solo Explorations (~2015-2019)
**Albums:** Flabby Wings, Big Bird at Nuremburg EP, Defenestrator, SoundCloud instrumentals
**Also:** Party Rock, Goodnight (demo)

- **Key centers:** C, G (Flabby Wings); C, E, B (Big Bird); C, D (Defenestrator)
- **Density:** Flabby Wings 8.9, Big Bird 6.8, Defenestrator 3.6
- **Character:** The widest range of any period. Solo material goes in two directions simultaneously:
  - **Ultra-dense:** Indefinite Diagnosis (17.2 n/s), Miserable Nostalgia (15.2), the Movement Suite (14.7 → 4.5 over 23 minutes)
  - **Near-ambient:** Defenestrator's Aquatic Amphetamines (1.0 n/s, 118 BPM), D.A.F.F.Y. (1.4 n/s, 132 BPM)

If you'd continued, the next Le Rug record might have been the most dynamic thing you'd made — alternating between ambient-sparse passages and ultra-dense walls.

### Phase 7: Maturity (2020)
**Album:** Anyone But Hindenburg

- **Key centers:** C, A, E — return to brighter centers
- **Density:** 9.1 n/s — your highest album average
- **Tempo:** 201 BPM — slightly pulled back from 2014
- **Character:** Longest songs you've written (4+ minutes). Most total notes. The interval analysis shows evolution — 4ths and 5ths became prominent melodic intervals for the first time, suggesting actual chord PROGRESSIONS rather than just power chord riffs. The pandemic isolation paradoxically produced your most musically sophisticated work.

The key center return to C/A/E (brighter, more open) after the dark E/A of 2014 suggests you came back to something — picking up a guitar after the solo electronics period and returning to your natural open-string instincts.

## The Real Story

It's not a simple arc from sparse to dense. It's a **V-shape with a tail:**

1. **Start dense** (Bleenex 8.3) — you arrived fully formed
2. **Pull back** (SRF 6.6, America 4.7, Sticky 3.5) — exploring, loosening
3. **Return to density** (DIAH 8.9, True Crime 9.2) — the comeback
4. **Simultaneous extremes** (Flabby Wings 17.2 high / Defenestrator 1.0 low) — the widest range
5. **Mature synthesis** (ABH 9.1) — dense but controlled, longest songs, most sophisticated

## Constants (What Never Changed)

Across every era and every project:
1. **Octave doubling** — always your #1 interval technique
2. **Note duration** — always ~0.25 seconds average
3. **E as a home note** — always top 5 in pitch class usage
4. **Power chords as foundation** — consistently the dominant chord type (46–71% across eras)
5. **Standard tuning** — your pitch class distribution always maps to EADGBE
6. **The zigzag** — melodies oscillate, they don't arc

## What Changed Most

1. **Density** swung from 3.5 (Sticky Buns) to 17.2 (Indefinite Diagnosis) — a 5x range
2. **Song length** grew (30-second miniatures → 4-minute epics → 15-minute noise pieces)
3. **Harmonic sophistication** increased (more 4th/5th movement in ABH)
4. **Key centers** shifted: A (Bleenex) → G/C (SRF) → E (middle) → C/A (ABH)
5. **Projects diversified** — from full bands to solo everything to hardware noise
6. **The medium changed** — eventually transitioned from music to board wargame design, a creative form with the same total-authorial-control dynamic but without the interpersonal friction of running a band
